# Visual benchmark summary

**Generated:** 2026-06-30  
**Project:** Champagne Christelle Phlipaux

## Comparison

| Reference              | Strongest lesson                      | Pattern not to copy                  |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Ruinart                | Photographic and editorial discipline | Institutional art-world scale        |
| Krug                   | A precise story for every bottle      | Prestige and rarity posture          |
| Bollinger              | Craft evidence tied to taste          | Heavy immersive platform             |
| Taittinger             | Family name as responsibility         | Dense institutional homepage         |
| Award-winning wineries | Whitespace, one image, one action     | Fashion effects and excessive motion |

## Opportunity

The clearest position is neither mass-market ecommerce nor inaccessible luxury:
a calm, authored website for a family grower in Channes. Its strongest proof is
the short distance between the vines, the cellar, Christelle’s advice and the
prepared order.

## Changes derived from the benchmark

- Simplify the homepage hero and remove repeated proof cards.
- Use a warm editorial surface to separate product discovery from the dark hero.
- Shorten copy that sounds abstract or overly polished.
- Keep product choices, prices and delivery information immediately legible.
- Strengthen local, descriptive page titles without keyword stuffing.
