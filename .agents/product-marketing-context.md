# Product marketing context — Champagne Christelle Phlipaux

## Company

Champagne Christelle Phlipaux is an independent family Champagne house located in Channes, in the Aube. The brand is built around proximity, sincere customer relationships, continuity of family know-how and pride in work carried out properly.

The business is not trying to imitate a large luxury house. Its strongest advantage is the opposite: customers can understand who produces the Champagne, how it is made and who they are buying from.

## Strategic objective

The website must support direct sales while strengthening the credibility and memorability of the House. It should turn curiosity into confidence, then confidence into a simple purchase.

The experience should answer four questions quickly:
1. Who is behind the Champagne?
2. Why should I trust this House?
3. Which cuvée or format should I choose?
4. How will ordering and delivery work?

## Priority audiences

### Direct customers

People buying Champagne for meals, celebrations, gifts or personal enjoyment. They may know little about technical wine vocabulary and need straightforward guidance.

Main needs:
- confidence in the producer
- understandable differences between cuvées
- visible prices and formats
- clear delivery rules
- reassurance about ordering bottles online

### Existing customers and referrals

Customers who already know the family, have purchased through word of mouth or use the depositary network.

Main needs:
- quickly find the familiar cuvée
- reorder without friction
- locate a nearby depositary
- verify current prices and formats

### Younger customers to develop

Adults who are interested in Champagne but may perceive the category as formal, inaccessible or old-fashioned.

Main needs:
- simple explanations
- authentic behind-the-scenes content
- approachable language
- modern mobile experience
- reasons to choose an independent producer

### Professional buyers

Restaurants, bars, caterers, retailers, companies and event professionals.

Main needs:
- credible presentation of the House
- easy contact route
- formats and range overview
- reassurance about reliability and human follow-up

Do not expose confidential professional pricing or commercial conditions unless explicitly approved.

## Positioning

A family Champagne that combines quality, sincerity and a strong relationship with customers.

Not:
- ostentatious luxury
- mass-market discount Champagne
- a fashionable lifestyle brand disconnected from production
- technical elitism

## Core differentiators

- independent family identity
- direct relationship with the producer
- real work in the vineyard and cellar
- approachable explanations
- established customer relationships
- depositary network in France
- fair, clearly displayed online prices

Never invent the age of the House, vineyard area, production volume, grape proportions, awards or environmental certifications without a verified source in the repository or explicit user approval.

## Brand personality

- sincere
- welcoming
- attentive
- grounded
- competent
- generous
- elegant through restraint

## Messaging pillars

### The people behind the bottle

Show the family, daily work, decisions and relationships that make the House tangible.

### Work done properly

Explain vineyard and cellar tasks in a concrete, accessible way. Demonstrate quality instead of proclaiming it.

### Champagne without unnecessary distance

Help customers understand, choose, serve and enjoy Champagne without intimidating jargon.

### A lasting customer relationship

Make contact, advice, deposits and repeat ordering visible advantages rather than secondary details.

## Customer objections to address

- “I cannot taste before ordering.”
- “I do not know which cuvée to choose.”
- “Will the bottles arrive safely?”
- “How much does delivery cost?”
- “Why buy from this producer rather than a known national brand?”
- “Can I find the Champagne near me?”
- “Is online payment trustworthy?”

Use factual answers. Do not fabricate guarantees or logistics promises.

## Conversion principles

- clarity before persuasion
- proof before superlatives
- visible total cost before checkout
- recommendations based on occasions and taste, not invented prestige
- one clear primary action per section
- keep contact possible for customers who prefer human advice

## Content opportunities

- seasonal vineyard work
- bottle preparation and labelling
- explanations of formats and cuvées
- serving and storage advice
- customer questions
- depositary highlights
- the life of an independent family House

Alcohol communication must remain responsible, adult-oriented and compliant with applicable French advertising rules. Never frame alcohol as necessary for success, seduction, performance, social acceptance or emotional relief.

## Approved current online prices

- Brut 75 cl: 22.50 €
- Brut magnum: 49 €
- Brut carton of 6: 135 €
- Rosé 75 cl: 25 €
- Rosé carton of 6: 150 €
- Demi-sec: same prices as Brut

These are website prices, not a claim about all sales channels or future pricing.

## Approved delivery logic

- 1 standard bottle: 12 € total
- 2 standard bottles: 10 € total
- 3 standard bottles: 6 € total
- 4 or 5 standard bottles: 10 € total
- free delivery from 6 standard bottles
- magnums: 10 € each, cumulative

When combinations create ambiguity, preserve existing tested production behavior and flag the question rather than guessing.

## Decision filter

Before approving any design or copy change, ask:
1. Does it make the House more credible and human?
2. Does it help a customer understand or choose?
3. Is every claim verifiable?
4. Does it remain simple on mobile?
5. Does it avoid generic luxury clichés?
6. Does it protect the purchasing path?
